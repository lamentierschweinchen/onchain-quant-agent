Contributing factor

Supernova activates on 10 September

8
days to epoch 2233

Block time drops from 6 seconds to 600 milliseconds. Intra-shard finality falls below 250ms, cross-shard settlement from ~18s to ~2.4s. Activation requires mainnet to stop accepting new pool transactions for about 24 minutes, after which queued transactions resume.

The node migration window opened on 1 September — the day this move began. It is the only specific catalyst identifiable, and no token-economic change accompanies it: no supply, staking or fee changes were announced.

A caveat on the catalyst. Scanning the node fleet for Supernova-capable software — a full sweep of 1,566 observers and a 2,100-validator sample — returned no nodes on the v2.x branch that carries the Supernova code. 92.8–98% sit on v1.11.11.0, the newest pre-Supernova tag. Stated as a question, not a conclusion: the release notes do not say which tag mainnet requires, so the production build may be a v1.11.x tag this scan cannot distinguish. Worth resolving before treating 10 September as certain.

What it adds up to

Three things at once, not one

The first version of this brief concluded that holders were selling into the bid. The decomposition above overturns that, and it is worth stating plainly: the whale-tier aggregates that produced it were measuring exchange wallets, not holders.

What the evidence actually supports is three things happening together. A heavily short derivatives book — 28.4% of market cap in open interest, funding negative on 27 of 32 venues — is being squeezed, and that is what produced a +17% session on 6.4x spot volume. Underneath it, spot demand is unusually broad: 365 wallets took delivery in 48 hours against 50 in the prior week. And into both, the OTC desks are delivering their record inventory, halved from 266,213 to 133,150.

The bull and bear cases are therefore about sequencing, not disagreement about facts. The squeeze is finite and ends when the short book is closed. The desk supply is finite too — 133,150 EGLD, and visible. Whether the broad spot bid outlasts both is the open question, and it is the genuinely new thing here: withdrawal breadth has never been this high in twenty-three runs of tracking.

The dated risk is Supernova itself. The move is three weeks older than the catalyst, the catalyst is eight days out, and everyone can see the date.
